# Who is the buyer? Who pays the agent?

## Week 2: measurement, NOT commerce

**Task issuer:** the research team. We write the six software specifications and the independent tests.

**Worker:** Codex, acting as a single coding agent. It receives instructions to implement `solution.py`.

**Payment:** none to the agent. We may consume Codex subscription allowance or pay actual API costs, depending on our authentication. A field named `hypothetical_contract_usd` is a made-up thought-experiment price, **not revenue** and not a customer willingness-to-pay observation. `net_income_usd` intentionally stays blank.

**Evidence collected:** pass/fail, resource usage, elapsed time, failures. This tests capability/cost; it does not validate a machine economy or a bank.

## Week 3: controlled toy commerce

A *contractor agent* gets an illustrative project budget. It may pay a specialized QA agent using a **simulated internal ledger** to improve its result. The accounting transfer moves units between agent accounts; **no external value is created** merely because they pay one another. Compare this setup to a single agent doing the same task with tools, under equal total resources.

## Later: real customer commerce (if research supports it)

An independent human/company buyer offers a real paid software task. An agent, **operated by a responsible legal person or business**, may complete it, with actual customer acceptance and payment through a compliant payment provider. The enterprise's net result is actual customer revenue minus API, cloud, human review, refunds, software and other real expenses. Funds and legal obligations remain with the responsible human/company, not an independently incorporated AI.

## Later still: financial research

Only after we observe an economically viable agent enterprise would we test whether lack of upfront capital prevents profitable work. We would compare customer deposits, delayed supplier payments, and financing, rather than presuming a bank is needed. Any initial credit test uses simulated balances, not real borrowing.

## Concrete example

A real customer pays a responsible contractor business **$100** after accepting a task. Coding inference costs $12; external QA costs $8. The business has **$80 before all other costs**, not an agent's personal bank balance. An internal simulation with a $100 *hypothetical* reward is **not** a $100 sale, even if the tests pass.

## Important experimental rule

Always distinguish (1) simulated transfers, (2) actual compute/API outlays, and (3) independently received customer revenue. Never add internal transfers to external sales or report hypothetical prices as earned income.
