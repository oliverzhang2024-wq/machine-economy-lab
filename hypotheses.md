# Hypothesis register — do not rewrite after seeing outcomes without a dated amendment

## H0: Single-agent production is measurable
A standard coding agent can complete some reproducible tasks; its outputs, usage, and failure modes can be recorded consistently.

**Disconfirmation:** unstable setup, irreproducible results, significant hidden human labor, or opaque costs.

## H1: Specialization can improve delivery (Week 3)
Under certain conditions, allocating programming and QA to separate agents may improve the success-rate-adjusted cost compared with a single general agent.

**Disconfirmation:** equal or worse pass rate, higher total token cost, duplicated effort, unresolved accountability.

## H2: Agents have transaction/verification frictions (Week 3–4)
Independent agents may need mechanisms to verify delivery and handle payment disputes when purchasing specialized work.

**Disconfirmation:** ordinary tool calls and human-owned contracts solve problems adequately, or multi-agent commerce creates no gain.

## H3: Productive agent enterprises may face financing gaps (future)
Some agents with real, independently paying customers may be unable to accept positive-value contracts because upfront compute and tool costs exceed accessible working capital.

**Disconfirmation:** customers pay upfront; resources are near-zero cost; operators can fund projects cheaply; financing makes economic results worse.

## H4: A new intermediary is needed (future)
A specialist financial institution may allocate capital/risk better than existing wallets, banks, cloud accounts, and payment processors.

**Disconfirmation:** conventional credit, billing, escrow, or platforms already solve the problem at lower cost and risk.

## Current claim strength
H0: subject of this week's pilot, no agent-run observations yet.
H1-H4: exploratory hypotheses, not established facts.
